# REGISTER 05 — EN addendum for A2-K7 M-014d1b

**Date:** 2026-07-13  
**Status:** binding addendum; existing rules are unchanged

## Duplicate check

AR20 separates bath types and AR21 separates correlation time from
interaction rate. They do not distinguish relativistic `2->2` bath
scattering from an emitting transition or define when high-frequency
spontaneous emission ceases to be a local KMS bath. AR23 and AR24 are not
duplicates.

## AR23 — A thermal-scattering rate is not a universal emission bound

An estimate derived for relativistic `2->2` processes at energy `T` must
not be applied to a decay, spontaneous/stimulated emission, or coherent
collective transition without a new derivation. Every channel requires its
own gap, matrix element, selection rules, form factor, and rate script. An
earlier over-broad verdict is retained with an erratum.

## AR24 — High-frequency spontaneous emission without reverse absorption is not a local KMS bath

If a required transition has `omega/T>>1`, reverse absorption is
Boltzmann-suppressed. It must not be used as a local thermal Onsager/KMS
bath, but moved to a vacuum or non-thermal coloured track with its own
memory/noise kernel. Coherent enhancement must be derived rather than
replaced by a cell count without a form factor.

## Q50 — Did the incoherent KMS graviton transition K1a2a survive?

**Status:** `NO — DEAD M-014d1b, Maximum depth 42/100.`

Within the optimistic envelope `Gamma=omega^3/Mbar_Pl^2`, the `omega<=T`
window misses a factor `2.2e26–3.7e33`. Meeting the rate without enhancement
would require `omega=10.8–229 MeV`, or `omega/T~6e8–1.5e11`; reverse
absorption is effectively zero. Coherent K1a2b remains open and the
high-frequency spontaneous branch moves to K2. K7 remains `30/100`.

### Limitation of older formulations

M-014d1 now applies only to the K1a1 thermal-scattering channel, not every
gravity-only transition. The numerical `M_eff` part of M-014d2a is also
specific to its thermal-scattering model; the soft-Ward death of leading
K1b1 remains valid.
