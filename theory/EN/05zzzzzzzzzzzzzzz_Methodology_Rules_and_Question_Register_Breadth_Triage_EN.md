# Addendum to 05 — counting live tracks and breadth triage (EN)

**Date:** 2026-07-14  
**Status:** binding addendum; older rules remain unchanged

## Duplication check

AR10 separates physically distinct tracks, AR18 prevents automatic parent
promotion, and AR30/AR43 define track depth. What was missing was a rule for
counting shallow “live” hypotheses and for deciding when a deep track should
yield to a breadth audit. AR44 fills this gap.

## AR44 — The live-track count must always be split by passed gate

A single total number of `live tracks` is prohibited unless it is split at
least into:

1. non-killed registered G1 hypotheses;
2. tracks that sequentially passed G2;
3. tracks that sequentially passed G3 or deeper.

`WAITING`, `not killed`, or G1 registration alone is not evidence of physical
viability. A breadth triage awards depth only through the same sequential
gates and may not use weaker criteria than the deep track.

The deepest active track is not interrupted before a cheap decision block
merely to increase the number of registered alternatives. Breadth triage is
performed before the next materially more expensive gate, upon physical
death, or when the same technical wall survives three consecutive bounded
revisions.

Tracks belonging to another A1 background branch must be counted separately.

## Q71 — Continue K4 or rapidly scan the unstarted tracks first?

**Answer:** use a hybrid. K4 continues through BR3C to C7.8/`68.0`, because
it is the only A1-K1 track beyond G3 and now has `66.2/100`. Before the
expensive BR4, perform a breadth triage of K8/K9 and a re-entry audit of
K7/K11/K12. If K4 dies physically, start the triage immediately.

A1-K1 currently has six non-killed tracks, two at least at G2, and only one
at least at G3. These three numbers must not be merged into a claim of “six
physically viable models”.
