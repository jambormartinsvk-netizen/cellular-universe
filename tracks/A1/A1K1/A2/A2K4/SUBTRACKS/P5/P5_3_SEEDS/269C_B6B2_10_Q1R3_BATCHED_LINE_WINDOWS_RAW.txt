L43@P1: 1 Introduction
L44@P1: The cosmological first-order phase transition (FOPT) and its associated phase transition
L45@P1: gravitational waves (GWs) open a new window to explore many fundamental problems in
L46@P1: particle cosmology, such as electroweak baryogenesis [1, 2] and dark matter [3–7], etc. During
L47@P1: an FOPT, GW signals can be generated by bubble collisions [8–10], sound waves [11–13], and
L48@P1: turbulence [14–16]. Future GW experiments, such as TianQin [17, 18], LISA [19], Taiji [20],
L49@P1: and Big Bang Observatory (BBO) [21], among others, may be able to detect phase transition
L50@P1: GW signals and reveal various puzzles about our Universe. Recent studies [11–13] have shown
L51@P1: that the sound wave is the dominant source of phase transition GWs in a thermal FOPT. To
L52@P1: finally pin down the underlying physics of an FOPT, we need precise quantification of the
L53@P1: GW spectra and their signal-to-noise ratio in future GW experiments. To that end, obtaining
L54@P1: accurate estimations of phase transition parameters that determine the GW spectra become
L55@P1: critical. The two most crucial variables among these parameters are the bubble wall velocity
L56@P1: and the energy budget, where the latter is also known as the kinetic energy fraction, which
L57@P1: characterizes the fraction of energy released during an FOPT that is transferred into the
L58@P1: kinetic energy of the ambient fluid.
L59@P1: Previous studies [22–28] on the energy budget of an FOPT are based on the conventional
L60@P1: model-independent method. This method matches a well-motivated particle physics model
L61@P1: to a specific model of equation of state (EoS) according to some phase transition parameters,
L62@P1: such as the strength parameter and the bubble wall velocity. As a result, the boundary
L63@P1: conditions of the fluid equations can be determined, and then the corresponding kinetic
L64@P1: energy fraction can be derived. However, the specific model of EoS, such as the bag model,
L65@P1-2: – 1 –cannot fully capture the features of the symmetric and broken phases since some important
L66@P2: factors are ignored, such as the variation of the sound speed of the plasma. To address these
L67@P2: limitations, some other models of EoS are constructed. For example, the µν model [24–27] can
L68@P2: take the variation of the sound speed into account by considering it as an extra parameter.
L69@P2: Additionally, to incorporate the temperature-dependent sound speed, a more complicated
L70@P2: model of EoS is built in ref. [28], which includes more extra parameters. Although the modelindependent method is convenient for studying various particle physics models, the accuracy
L71@P2: of this method depends on the constructed EoS model. To capture all relevant properties, a
L72@P2: complicated EoS model should be considered, and sophisticated calculations could be involved.
L73@P2: Furthermore, the calculation of the bubble wall velocity is decoupled from the kinetic energy
L74@P2: fraction in the conventional model-independent method.
